What the MIT licence means for GoEasyERP

15 November 2026

GoEasyERP is open source and under the MIT licence, one of the most permissive licences there is.

What does that actually mean?

The MIT licence is one of the shortest and most widely used software licences in the world. It takes its name from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where it was first written in the 1980s, and today it covers well-known software such as React, Node.js and Ruby on Rails.

In plain words, it gives everyone the right to:

  • use the software free of charge, for any purpose, including commercially
  • read and change the code
  • copy it, share it and run it wherever they choose
  • build their own products on top of it

It asks for only one thing in return: the copyright notice and the licence text must stay with the code. The software also comes “as is”, without a warranty, which is standard for open-source licences.

The MIT licence is called permissive because, unlike some other open-source licences, it does not require you to publish your own changes. A business can adapt GoEasyERP to its own needs and keep those adaptations private.

For a GoEasyERP user, that means no licence fees, no per-user charges and no lock-in. We earn our living from consulting hours, not licences.

The licence and the code are on GitHub.
